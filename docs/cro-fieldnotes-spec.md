# Indizio CRO Fieldnotes — Living Product Specification

**Status:** Initial experience built; editorial system evolving  
**Last updated:** August 22, 2026

## 1. Purpose

CRO Fieldnotes publishes Indizio's original research about ecommerce brands, industries, conversion decisions, and emerging blueprints. These are evidence-led articles and teardowns, not generic marketing posts.

## 2. Content types

- Brand teardown
- Industry blueprint
- Conversion observation
- Experiment analysis
- Emerging ecommerce behavior
- Comparative research across multiple brands

Fieldnotes may have longer, editorial titles and should support substantial article length.

## 3. Index page

The `/fieldnotes` page should resemble the clarity of the Website Library while using one full-width row per article. Each row contains:

- Industry tag
- Long title
- Short description
- Publication or reading metadata when useful
- Clear link to the complete article

Future controls can include industry, topic, article type, and newest/featured sorting. Only add filters when the catalogue is large enough to benefit from them.

## 4. Article page

Each article should include:

- Descriptive title and standfirst
- Author and publication information
- Industry and topic tags
- Reading-progress bar directly below the sticky navigation
- Structured headings and readable long-form typography
- Images, annotated evidence, and captions where appropriate
- Links to relevant library websites
- Related Fieldnotes and Ecommerce Ideas
- Save/highlight action for signed-in users
- Newsletter CTA and shared site footer

The announcement bar is not sticky. The reduced-height main navigation remains sticky and visible immediately.

## 5. Payload content model

Recommended fields:

- Title and slug
- Short description
- Hero or social cover
- Rich article content
- Article type
- Industries and topics
- Related websites
- Related articles
- Author
- Status and publication date
- Featured rank
- SEO title and description
- Canonical URL
- Estimated reading time

Use drafts and editorial preview before publication.

## 6. Saving and highlighting

Fieldnotes are managed separately from website saves at the content level, even if they share account infrastructure. A user should be able to save a complete Fieldnote. Highlighting individual passages is a future capability and requires anchors that remain stable when the article is edited.

Potential future organization:

- Saved Fieldnotes
- Article collections
- Highlights with private notes
- Exportable research summaries

Do not mix article saves into public website save counts.

## 7. SEO and editorial standards

- Publish original research and specific evidence.
- Use one stable canonical URL per article.
- Provide unique metadata and meaningful headings.
- Link claims to the relevant storefront examples.
- Add Article structured data when the implementation is ready.
- Include articles in XML sitemaps only after publication.
- Avoid mass-producing shallow posts merely to target keywords.

## 8. Success metrics

- Organic entrances
- Article completion and scroll depth
- Return visits
- Fieldnote saves
- Newsletter subscriptions attributed to articles
- Outbound visits to referenced websites
- Links and shares earned

## 9. Near-term roadmap

2. Replace placeholder Fieldnotes with original research.
3. Add save functionality for articles.
4. Add related websites and related article blocks.
5. Add SEO metadata, structured data, and sitemap coverage.
6. Consider passage highlighting only after article editing behavior is stable.
